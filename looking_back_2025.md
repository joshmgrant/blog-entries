Another year has past, and so I'm going to take the time to look back at 2025. It's been a wild one, to say the least.

## General
- One thing is certain about the rise of AI in our world: smart people _love_ talking about it.
- Eventually people figure out that authoritarian dictatorships are, in fact, not good at all.
- It is incredibly difficult to get people to do things.
- It seems trite, but I do believe art and artists will lead the way on many, many things.

## Tech & Software
- The current AI craze is extreme because, in the words of Paul Ford, AI is touching a lot of hot stoves at once. AI is both tech and highly sociological.
- Despite the previous point, I am cautiously optimistic that LLM-based tools will gradually become commonplace in software engineering, and that this will be overall a good thing.
- I still enjoy using Python. Recently I started using [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/) for some personal projects and it's pretty good. Python may be irksome for professional software projects, but it's great for writing code in many contexts inside and away from software engineering.
- Never bet against the web, since you'll likely lose.
- I still really [hate authentication](https://simplythetest.tumblr.com/post/776761265732419584/i-hate-authentication). My opinion is that too many things in life are "gated" unnecessarily, and you may feel free to disagree with me.
- It's a strange time to be in software quality, in particular test automation. I've heard that the rise of AI will mean that quality/test engineer rolls will be more critical than ever, along with hearing that QA folks can be effectively replaced with AI. Not sure what to make of this, to be honest other than yes, we will need (human) quality engineers in the future.
  
## Canadiana
- The Toronto Blue Jays had a marvelous season and post-season and came within metaphorical centimeters of winning the World Series. Honestly, this was great for everyone in Canada, and a lot of people outside of Canada too.
- With the election of Donald Trump and the resulting political situation I'm still not planning on traveling to the United States any time soon. I will say part of me misses going to the US, even just a bit.
- To provide some of my personal experience of how US-Canada tensions are affecting everyday life, some speakers coming from the US cancelled last minute for a local software testing conference in Cambridge, Ontario, causing some chaos for organizers. 
- Poutine is still delicious, if decadent.
- I've finally found a good reason to visit the Prairie provinces: great fine art. I hope to go to places like Winnipeg or Regina for this reason.
- Even as someone who's worked in tech for many years, creating a federal Minister of AI seems objectively strange to me.
- Similarly, data sovereignty is something Canada should figure out sooner than later.
- An up and coming "cool" part of Ontario could be Cambridge/Galt. I didn't believe this years ago but my wife said so and it turns out she may be right.

## Personal
- This year I turned 40, and let me tell you: my body made this very clear.
- One thing that I've learned being a parent is that things often come and go. You may never know the last time you'll change a diaper or when you don't need a car seat anymore. Things change.
- As this was another year where I find myself looking for work, I've been reflecting on the nature of labour, and in particular on [art and labour](https://medium.com/@joshin4colours/art-and-leisure-the-labour-of-labour-6a070f43d5e4). In a nutshell: not all work is valued the way we think it should be.
- Although AI tools have many pros and cons when it comes to impacting software development, it does remind me that learning the fundamentals is extremely valuable.
